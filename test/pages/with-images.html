Field notes from the paper mill

The mill runs three shifts and has done since nineteen seventy-four, which is longer than most of the people working it have been alive. What follows is an account of a single Tuesday inside it, written down because nobody had asked the question before and the answers turned out to be more interesting than the premise deserved.

Pulp arrives wet and leaves dry, and almost everything between those two states is a negotiation with heat. The operators talk about the machine the way sailors talk about weather: not as something they control but as something they read, and the reading takes about a decade to learn properly.

By the end of the shift the floor is loud enough that conversation happens entirely in hand signals, a vocabulary of maybe forty gestures that no one has ever written down and that every new hire absorbs within a month or leaves.
